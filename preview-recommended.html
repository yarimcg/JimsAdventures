The pitch

A climbing trip you’ll talk about for years

Kawakawa Bay sits on the north-western shore of Lake Taupō — rhyolite cliffs in native bush, a grassy camp on the water, and swimming so good you’ll forget the last pitch. Jim runs these as custom, two-person tours. Not a group. Not a timetable you have to squeeze into. Your goals, his rack, a stove that never stops.

01

Hike in — it’s the simplest way

You can ride, kayak, or take a water taxi — Jim recommends we hike. It’s a beautiful walk, and it sets the scene of a calm adventure. From Kinloch, the K2K track takes about two hours — bush, bluffs, then the drop into the bay. Packs on, lake getting bigger with every saddle. You walk into camp. No marina circus. Just the sound of water and the first look at the walls you’ll live under for a few days.

02

Camp on the lake

The venue is a grassy camp on the shore — on the rim of a supervolcano, rhyolite at your back. Pitch your tent. Swim whenever the rock gets too warm. All drinking water is filtered. Jim cooks the three main meals — a custom healthy menu, dehydrated before we arrive, then cooked in camp. You bring your own snacks for the wall and the walk.

03

Then the rock starts

Long moderate routes. Multi-pitch above the water. Traditional cracks if that’s the itch. Jim brings the full lead rack — cams, wires, draws, the works. You bring your own helmet, harness, rope, and shoes. He’ll put you on the sharp end, or he’ll lead every pitch while you follow in the sun. Your call. The online climbing guide has the routes.

04

By night, by the lake

You sleep in your own tent, with your own sleeping gear. When the cliff goes gold, Jim fires the stove. Filtered water, a proper feed, and the lake going quiet under the trees. This is the part people don’t put on a climbing résumé — and the part you’ll talk about first when you get home. Walk out the same way you came. A nice café at Kinloch finishes the trip.

Your host

Jim Hagger

Guide.

Jim has been guiding all sorts of trips for over 20 years. He’ll hike you into Kawakawa Bay, put you on the sharp end or lead every pitch himself, then feed you properly until the lake goes black.

His main gig is high-school teaching, so there’s always a bit of juggling — and always room for flexibility. Off the rock he’s the same animal: bikes, bush, a dusty trail and a thumbs-up.

The walk in

Kinloch to Kawakawa Bay

It is possible to ride, kayak, or take a water taxi, but Jim recommends we hike. It’s a beautiful walk, and it sets the scene of a calm adventure. About 9 km on the K2K track — two hours on foot. The camp sits at the water, cliffs at the eastern end of the bay. Taupō township is a 30-minute drive from the Kinloch trailhead. Walk out, then sit down — a nice café at Kinloch finishes the trip.

  • A Little Harbour carpark — café at the finish
  • B Kawakawa camp — tents, swim, kitchen
  • C Codger’s Rock — lookout on the walk

Aotearoa

Where in New Zealand?

Kawakawa Bay sits on the north-western shore of Lake Taupō — the big volcanic lake in the middle of the North Island. Fly into Auckland or Taupō. Airport pick-up and drop-off is available — the idea is to keep things simple. Ask for what you need.

  • ● Kawakawa Bay — the camp
  • Auckland — about 3½ hours’ drive
  • Taupō town — about 40 minutes to Kinloch
  • Wellington — about 4½ hours’ drive

When to come

When is the best time of year?

Summer is the best time of year — long days, warm rock, a swim after. Open dates follow NZ school holidays and public holidays. Winter can be cold and the daylight hours are short. Public holidays get busy, but we’ll always make something work. Each trip is two nights and three days.
