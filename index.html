The pitch

A climbing trip built like a secret.

Kawakawa Bay sits on the north-western shore of Lake Taupō — rhyolite cliffs in native bush, a grassy camp on the water, and swimming so good you’ll forget the last pitch. Jim runs these as custom, two-person tours. Not a group. Not a timetable you have to squeeze into. Your goals, his rack, a stove that never stops.

01

Hike in. It’s the simplest way.

From Kinloch, the K2K track takes about two hours — bush, bluffs, then the drop into the bay. Packs on, lake getting bigger with every saddle. You walk into camp. No marina circus. Just the sound of water and the first look at the walls you’ll live under for a few days.

02

Camp on the lake.

The venue is a grassy camp on the shore — one of the North Island’s largest lakes stretching out in front of the rhyolite. Pitch your tent. Swim whenever the rock gets too warm. All drinking water is filtered. Jim runs the kitchen for every meal, breakfast through the late sitting after the last belay comes down.

03

Then the rock starts.

Long moderate routes. Multi-pitch above the water. Traditional cracks if that’s the itch. Jim brings the full lead rack — cams, wires, draws, the works. You bring your own helmet, harness, rope, and shoes. He’ll put you on the sharp end, or he’ll lead every pitch while you follow in the sun. Your call.

04

Nights with a guitar.

When the cliff goes gold, Jim serenades the bay. Filtered water, a proper feed, and six strings under the trees. This is the part people don’t put on a climbing résumé — and the part you’ll talk about first when you get home.

Made for you

Every trip is custom. Bring a goal.

Learning to lead. First multi-pitch. Traditional crack climbing. Or you want someone to lead you the whole way so you can look at the lake. Jim shapes the days around that.

Learn to lead

Place gear, clip bolts, manage the sharp end with someone who is actually watching — not shouting from the picnic table.

Multi-pitch

Belay changeovers, hanging stances, the long view. Two people is the comfortable number up there. That’s the cap, always.

Trad cracks

The bay is famous for trustworthy rhyolite and a high count of moderate gear routes. A beautiful place to learn to climb on cams and wires.

Follow Jim

You don’t have to be working a skill. If you want a holiday where someone else leads every pitch, that’s a real trip too.

Party size

02

Maximum two people.

Not a suggestion. On multi-pitch especially, two is simply more comfortable for everyone — the belays, the camp, the meals, the soundtrack. Come as a pair, or come solo and have Jim to yourself.

Your host

Jim Hagger.

Guide. Cook. Evening guitar.

Jim is why these trips stay at two people. He’ll hike you into Kawakawa Bay, put you on the sharp end or lead every pitch himself, then feed you properly and play until the lake goes black.

Off the rock he’s the same animal — bikes, bush, a dusty trail and a thumbs-up. The bay is his idea of a good week: a small party, a big lake, and no one shouting from the picnic table.

If you’ve got a goal — lead, multi-pitch, trad cracks — tell him. If you just want someone to take you up and cook, tell him that too.

Prime season runs October through April. Each block is a 3-night camp (hike in, two full days on the rock, hike out) unless you ask for something longer. Grey months are off-season — weather and Jim’s calendar both go quiet.

The walk in

Kinloch to Kawakawa Bay.

About 9 km on the K2K track. Two hours on foot, less if you ride. The camp sits at the water, cliffs at the eastern end of the bay. Taupō township is a 30-minute drive from the Kinloch trailhead.

  • A Kinloch — hike start
  • B Kawakawa camp — tents, swim, kitchen
  • C The cliffs — lead, multi-pitch, trad
  • D Codger’s Rock — lookout on the walk

Aotearoa

Where this is in New Zealand.

Kawakawa Bay sits on the north-western shore of Lake Taupō — the big volcanic lake in the middle of the North Island. Fly into Auckland or Taupō, drive to Kinloch, then walk in.

  • ● Kawakawa Bay — the camp
  • Auckland — about 3½ hours’ drive
  • Taupō town — about 40 minutes to Kinloch
  • Wellington — about 4½ hours’ drive
